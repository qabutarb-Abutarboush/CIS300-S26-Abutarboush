The Investiture Conflict was one of the most important power struggles in medieval Europe. It centered on the question of who had the authority to appoint church officials, especially bishops. This conflict brought the Church and political rulers into direct tension, with both sides fighting for control.

At the center of the conflict were Pope Gregory VII and Emperor Henry IV. Their disagreement led to major events, including Henry’s excommunication and his journey to Canossa. These moments showed how powerful the Church had become, even over kings.

The conflict ultimately changed the relationship between church and state. It helped establish clearer boundaries between religious and political authority, shaping European governance for centuries.
