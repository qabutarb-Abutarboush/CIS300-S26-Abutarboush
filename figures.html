Major Figures

Pope Gregory VII

Pope Gregory VII was one of the most important leaders in the Investiture Conflict. He believed that only the Church had the authority to appoint bishops and other church officials. His reforms challenged the traditional power of kings and emperors, especially Henry IV, and helped turn the dispute into a major struggle over authority in medieval Europe.

Henry IV

Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor, defended the traditional right of rulers to appoint bishops within their territories. He believed church appointments were an important part of royal authority and political control. His conflict with Gregory VII led to excommunication, rebellion among German nobles, and the famous journey to Canossa.

Matilda of Tuscany

Matilda of Tuscany was one of Pope Gregory VII’s strongest allies. As a powerful ruler in northern and central Italy, she supported the papacy with military strength, political protection, and territory that served as a safe base for papal supporters. Her backing gave Gregory VII crucial support during the conflict.

Wibert of Ravenna

Wibert of Ravenna was an important supporter of Henry IV during the Investiture Conflict. He opposed Gregory VII and became a central figure on the imperial side of the struggle. Later, he was made antipope Clement III, which deepened the conflict and showed how divided the Church and empire had become.

Archbishop of Mainz

The Archbishop of Mainz represented the importance of powerful church leaders within the Holy Roman Empire. As one of the most influential bishoprics in Germany, Mainz played a major role in both politics and religion. Leaders in positions like this were deeply involved in the struggle because bishops were not only spiritual officials, but also major political figures.
